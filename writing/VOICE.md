# VOICE.md

How Chase's sentences sound. Syntax, diction, and tone only; structure and substance live in `STYLE.md`.

## Scope

- **Writer:** Chase Beach
- **Applies to:** personal emails, civic letters, and bylined pieces such as the Mariemont Town Crier
- **Version:** v1, written 2026-10-15 from Chase's sent emails (Aug to Oct 2026) and his saved style preferences. Recalibrate with `cw-onboarding` after his first Town Crier draft, since an article runs longer than an email.

## Voice at its best

- Warm without gushing. He sounds glad to be writing to you, then gets to the point.
- Plain without being flat. Everyday words, plus one specific detail that shows he was paying attention.
- Upbeat and direct. Exclamation points are welcome; hedging is not.
- Humble and a little self-aware ("Part of me still kicks myself that...").

## Diction and register

### Favor

- Everyday, spoken words: "a lot," "really enjoyed," "get a kick out of it," "man" (with friends).
- Concrete specifics: real names, years, places, product names ("on your front steps with the dog," "the AS Colour Canvas Cord Collar Jacket (5533) in Army").
- First person and direct address. He writes to someone, not about something.
- Local names said the way locals say them: the Village, Kusel Stadium, Southerby's.

### Avoid

- Inflated or corporate words: "delighted," "leverage," "synergy," "excited to announce," "journey."
- Stacked pleasantries and filler thank-yous. One warm line is enough.
- Generic closers like "Hope our paths cross again soon."
- Model vocabulary: "delve," "tapestry," "testament," "nestled," "vibrant," "a true," "not just X but Y."

## Syntax and rhythm

- **Sentence length:** short to medium, mostly one idea per sentence. Long sentences only for a run of specifics.
- **Paragraph shape:** two to four sentences. Short paragraphs in emails; prose paragraphs in articles.
- **Punctuation:** never em dashes, and no en dashes as sentence punctuation. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead. Exclamation points are fine, about one per short email. An emoji is fine in a family or friendly email, never in an article.
- **Lists:** bullets when the content has real structure (options, steps, several asks), often with bold headers and nested sub-bullets. Prose for anything warm or personal.
- **Transitions:** plain connectors ("Alongside that," "Also,"). No "Moreover," "Furthermore," or "That said."

## Signature moves

| Move | What it does | When to use it | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| The warm one-line opener | "I hope you both had a wonderful summer and are gearing up for fall." Sets a friendly tone in one breath | Opening personal emails | Stacking two or three pleasantries |
| The specific callback | One concrete shared memory or detail ("when we met playing golf at the Presidio!") | Any note to someone he knows | Vague warmth with no detail |
| Context in one quick paragraph | Brings a reader up to date fast (sold InsurGrid, moved home, daughter born, took over Hugga) | Reconnecting, intros | Turning it into a résumé |
| The clean ask | Ends on one plain question ("Do you have any availability for a quick call next week?") | Any email that needs something | Burying the ask or asking three things at once |
| The sign-off | "Have a great day," / "Have a nice afternoon," then "Chase" | Emails | "Best," or "Warm regards," |

## Tics and patterns to avoid

| Pattern | Why it weakens the writing | Preferred response |
|---|---|---|
| Em dash | Not his voice and reads as AI-generated | Period, comma, colon, or parentheses |
| "I just wanted to reach out to..." | Throat-clearing | Start with the point or the warm line |
| Summary closer that restates the email | Padding | End on the ask or the sign-off |
| Three adjectives in a row | Sounds like marketing | One precise adjective, or a fact instead |

## Positive examples

Full emails live in `examples/chase-emails-2026.md`.

### Specific detail carries the warmth

> I pulled it up and there you were, right at the top of the page, on your front steps with the dog!

**Why it works:** one exact image does the work an adjective would try to do, and the exclamation point is earned.

### Honest, self-aware aside

> Part of me still kicks myself that I turned down having Packy write an article about InsurGrid in the fall 2020 before he blew up.

**Why it works:** admits something small and human, which makes the ask that follows feel personal.

## Negative examples

### Over-polished draft opener

> I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to take a moment to reach out and thank you for making it such a fun day.

**What is wrong:** two pleasantries and a filler thank-you before anything is said.

**Preferred move:** "Hey John," plus one specific callback, then the point.

## Voice review questions

- Would Chase say this sentence out loud to a neighbor?
- Is there at least one specific, real detail?
- Any em dashes, model vocabulary, or stacked pleasantries left?
- Does it end on a clear ask or a clean sign-off?
