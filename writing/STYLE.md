# STYLE.md

What Chase's writing must do, contain, and prove. Argument, evidence, structure, and readiness only; sentence sound lives in `VOICE.md`.

## Writing identity

- **Body of work:** Chase's personal and civic writing: Mariemont Town Crier pieces, letters to the Village and other officials, and personal emails.
- **What it is for:** connecting people in a small community, and getting practical things done (a crosswalk fixed, a meeting set, a thank-you said).
- **What makes it distinct:** written by a neighbor who grew up in Mariemont and came home. Personal knowledge plus checked facts.

## Audience context

- **Town Crier readers:** Mariemont residents of every age, many of whom know the people in the story personally. They read in print and online at their own pace.
- **Civic letters:** Village council, officials, and agency staff who are busy and need the facts and the ask fast.
- Put the assignment-specific reader in each piece's `drafts/<slug>/brief.md`.

## Reader promise

Every piece should give the reader:

- Something true and specific they did not know, even about people they know.
- A reason to feel good about the community, without pretending everything is perfect.
- A clear point or ask they can act on (for civic and personal writing).

## Evidence and provenance

- Every name, date, title, record, and statistic is checked against a source before it runs. Keep sources with the claim in `drafts/<slug>/research.md`.
- Spellings of people's names are confirmed with the person or an official roster (the coaches piece already has an Eric/Erik question).
- Quotes are checked word for word against the audio recording, not just a transcript.
- Anything personal or sensitive (a retirement not yet announced, a health detail) is cleared with the subject before it prints.
- Keep these separate: what the subject said, what records show, and what Chase remembers.

## Primary structure: Town Crier tribute or profile

1. **Open on a scene or moment,** ideally one Chase saw at the interview or remembers firsthand. Not a résumé line.
2. **Who they are and why now:** the news hook (for example, their final season) within the first two paragraphs.
3. **The record in one paragraph:** tenure, titles, numbers. This is the factual backbone.
4. **The people story:** two or three moments, each built around a quote. What they meant to players, students, families.
5. **Chase's own connection,** briefly and honestly, without making the piece about him.
6. **Close on their words or what comes next,** not on a summary.

**Length:** about 700 to 900 words with 2 to 3 photos, unless the editor says otherwise.

**Do not force it when:** the piece is news or a civic update. Then lead with the news and keep it shorter.

## Alternate structure: civic or advocacy letter

1. The ask, in one sentence.
2. The facts that make the case, bulleted, with the single strongest number in bold.
3. What has already been tried or who has been contacted.
4. A specific next step and a date.

## Openings

Strong openings:

- Put the reader in a place or moment (the bench at Kusel Stadium, a practice in the rain).
- Name the stakes or news by the second paragraph.

Avoid:

- "For X years, so-and-so has..." résumé openings.
- Opening with a dictionary definition, a question to the reader, or a quote with no setup.

## Endings

Strong endings:

- Give the subject the last word, or point to what happens next for them.
- Return to the opening scene with something changed.

Avoid:

- Summary endings ("In conclusion, they will be missed").
- Generic inspiration.

## Writing anti-patterns

| Pattern | Why it does not belong | Better move |
|---|---|---|
| A list of accomplishments with no people in it | Reads like a plaque, not a story | Tie each fact to a moment or a quote |
| Chase as the main character | The piece is about the subjects | One short paragraph of connection, then step back |
| Unverified records or quotes | Small towns notice mistakes | Check against a source or the recording |
| Two stories in one piece | Splits the reader's attention | Pick one; save the other for a follow-up |

## Publication readiness

### Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] Every name spelled right and confirmed.
- [ ] Every number, date, and title checked against a source.
- [ ] Every quote checked against the audio.
- [ ] Sensitive details cleared with the subject.
- [ ] Within the editor's word count, with photos and captions.
- [ ] The opening is a scene or moment, and the news is in the first two paragraphs.
- [ ] Checked against `VOICE.md`, with no em dashes.
